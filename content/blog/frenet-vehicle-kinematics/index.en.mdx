## Basic terminology and definition of the Frenet state

This article considers only vehicle **kinematics**: it describes how position and heading change with motion, without constructing a dynamic model of tire forces, load transfer, vehicle mass, or similar effects. The vehicle moves in a plane, and the center of its rear axle is used as the position reference point.

A **state** here is a set of variables that describes the vehicle's geometric position and orientation relative to the reference path and evolves with an independent variable. An **input** is supplied from outside the model and determines the state derivatives. A model whose independent variable is time $t$ is called a **time-domain model**. A model whose independent variable is the reference-path arc length $s$ is called a **reference-path-arc-length-domain model**, or, more briefly, a spatial-domain model.

The reference path is a $C^2$ planar curve parameterized by arc length $s$:

$$
\mathbf r:I\longrightarrow\mathbb R^2,
\qquad
s\longmapsto\mathbf r(s).
$$

Here, $I$ is the range of the arc-length parameter $s$. Give the Cartesian plane its standard orientation and let $J$ denote counterclockwise rotation by $90^\circ$. The reference path's unit tangent, left unit normal, signed curvature, and continuously unwrapped heading angle are denoted respectively by $\mathbf T$, $\mathbf N$, $\kappa_r$, and $\theta_r$. The following planar signed Frenet relations are used:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf T(s)
&=
\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf r}{\mathrm{d}s},&
\mathbf N(s)
&=
J\mathbf T(s),\\[0.6em]
\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf T}{\mathrm{d}s}
&=
\kappa_r\mathbf N,&
\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf N}{\mathrm{d}s}
&=
-\kappa_r\mathbf T,\\[0.6em]
\kappa_r
&=
\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta_r}{\mathrm{d}s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The curvature sign follows the left-normal convention: when traversing the reference path in the direction of increasing $s$, a left turn has $\kappa_r>0$ and a right turn has $\kappa_r<0$. The complete construction of arc length, frames, standard orientation, and these formulas appears in [Planar Frenet Frame](../planar-frenet-frame/). This article uses that construction as the geometric foundation of the vehicle kinematics.

Let $\mathbf z(t)$ denote the position vector of the rear-axle center in a fixed planar coordinate system, and let $\theta(t)$ be the vehicle heading angle. Define the corresponding heading unit vector by

$$
\mathbf e_\theta(t):=
\begin{bmatrix}
\cos\theta(t)\\
\sin\theta(t)
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Let $v(t)$ be the signed longitudinal velocity, positive in the $\mathbf e_\theta$ direction. **No sideslip** means that the instantaneous velocity of the rear-axle center has no component perpendicular to the vehicle's longitudinal axis:

$$
\dot{\mathbf z}=v\mathbf e_\theta.
$$

Thus, $v>0$ when driving forward, $v<0$ when reversing, and $v=0$ when stationary. The vector $\mathbf e_\theta$ always describes the vehicle-body orientation; the actual direction of velocity while the vehicle is moving also depends on the sign of $v$.

Let $\ell$ be the **oriented arc-length coordinate** of the rear-axle-center trajectory. Given an initial value $\ell(t_0)$, define

$$
\ell(t)=\ell(t_0)+\int_{t_0}^{t}v(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau,
$$

so that

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}\ell}{\mathrm{d}t}=v.
$$

The value of $\ell$ increases while driving forward and decreases while reversing. It is not the monotonically increasing cumulative distance, whose increment would instead be computed from $|v|\,\mathrm{d}t$. On a trajectory segment where $v\ne0$, $\ell$ can serve as a local parameter. The chain rule then gives $\mathrm{d}\mathbf z/\mathrm{d}\ell=\mathbf e_\theta$, so the signed curvature of the vehicle trajectory under this orientation is defined by

$$
\kappa_v
:=
\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{\mathrm{d}\ell}.
$$

For a given vehicle position, the **closest-point projection** finds the point on the reference path that minimizes the distance to the rear-axle center. This article assumes that the projection is unique and lies in the interior of the reference-path parameter interval, so the line from the projection point to the rear-axle center is perpendicular to the path. Denote the projection point's arc-length coordinate by $s(t)$. Let $d(t)$ be the signed distance from that point to the vehicle along the left unit normal $\mathbf N$: $d>0$ places the vehicle to the left of the increasing-$s$ direction, and $d<0$ places it to the right. Define the difference between the vehicle heading and the reference heading at the projection point by

$$
\varphi(t)
:=
\theta(t)-\theta_r\bigl(s(t)\bigr).
$$

Both $\theta$ and $\theta_r$ are continuously unwrapped along the trajectory segment under consideration, preventing an artificial jump in $\varphi$ when an angle representation crosses the $\pm\pi$ boundary. The time-domain model uses time $t$ as its independent variable and $(s,d,\varphi)$ as its state. The reference-path-arc-length-domain, or spatial-domain, model instead uses $s$ as its independent variable and therefore requires only $(d,\varphi)$ as its state. Figure 1 shows the geometric relationship among these quantities. The vectors $\mathbf T(s)$ and $\mathbf N(s)$ are the Frenet basis vectors at the projection point; the purple bidirectional dimension line is the lateral deviation $d$ from the reference path to the rear-axle center; and the arrow $\mathbf e_\theta$ at the rear-axle center is the vehicle heading unit vector.

Throughout this article, upright $\mathrm{d}$ denotes the differential symbol, distinct from the italic lateral-deviation variable $d$; $\dot d$ denotes the time derivative of that deviation.

## Time-domain Frenet kinematics

### Position-coordinate kinematics

From the normal-coordinate representation in Figure 1, the vehicle position is

$$
\mathbf z(t) =
\mathbf r\bigl(s(t)\bigr)
+
d(t)\mathbf N\bigl(s(t)\bigr).
\tag{1}
$$

Differentiate Equation (1) with respect to time $t$, using $\mathrm{d}\mathbf r/\mathrm{d}s=\mathbf T$ and $\mathrm{d}\mathbf N/\mathrm{d}s=-\kappa_r\mathbf T$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dot{\mathbf z}
&=
\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf r}{\mathrm{d}s}\dot s
+
\dot d\,\mathbf N
+
d\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf N}{\mathrm{d}s}\dot s\\[0.6em]
&=
\mathbf T\dot s
+
\dot d\,\mathbf N -
d\kappa_r\mathbf T\dot s\\[0.6em]
&=
(1-d\kappa_r)\dot s\,\mathbf T
+
\dot d\,\mathbf N.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here and below, any $\mathbf T$, $\mathbf N$, and $\kappa_r$ without an explicit argument are evaluated at the projection point $s(t)$. The no-sideslip assumption gives $\dot{\mathbf z}=v\mathbf e_\theta$. This relation remains valid for both $v>0$ while driving forward and $v<0$ while reversing. Since $(\mathbf T,\mathbf N)$ is an orthonormal basis, $\mathbf e_\theta$ can be expanded by its orthogonal projections onto the two basis directions:

$$
\mathbf e_\theta =
\langle\mathbf e_\theta,\mathbf T\rangle\mathbf T
+
\langle\mathbf e_\theta,\mathbf N\rangle\mathbf N.
$$

Substituting

$$
\mathbf T =
\begin{bmatrix}
\cos\theta_r\\
\sin\theta_r
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\mathbf N =
\begin{bmatrix}
-\sin\theta_r\\
\cos\theta_r
\end{bmatrix}
$$

into the projection coefficients gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle\mathbf e_\theta,\mathbf T\rangle
&=
\cos\theta\cos\theta_r
+
\sin\theta\sin\theta_r\\
&=
\cos(\theta-\theta_r) =
\cos\varphi,\\[0.7em]
\langle\mathbf e_\theta,\mathbf N\rangle
&=
-\cos\theta\sin\theta_r
+
\sin\theta\cos\theta_r\\
&=
\sin(\theta-\theta_r) =
\sin\varphi.
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the vehicle heading unit vector has the following representation in the Frenet frame:

$$
\mathbf e_\theta =
\cos\varphi\,\mathbf T
+
\sin\varphi\,\mathbf N.
$$

The vehicle velocity is consequently

$$
\dot{\mathbf z} =
v\mathbf e_\theta =
v\cos\varphi\,\mathbf T
+
v\sin\varphi\,\mathbf N.
$$

Using the same Frenet frame and the same resultant velocity vector, Figure 2 separately shows the two components obtained by differentiating the position coordinates and by expressing the no-sideslip velocity. Placing them side by side makes the subsequent coefficient comparison a direct geometric correspondence.

Equating the coefficients of the same velocity vector in the basis $(\mathbf T,\mathbf N)$ gives

$$
(1-d\kappa_r)\dot s =
v\cos\varphi,
\qquad
\dot d =
v\sin\varphi.
$$

When $1-d\kappa_r\ne0$, the time-domain position kinematics are

$$
\dot s =
\frac{v\cos\varphi}{1-d\kappa_r},
\qquad
\dot d =
v\sin\varphi.
$$

The normal coordinates and velocity decomposition of Equation (1) appear in [[1, §2.1–2.2, Eqs. (14)–(20)]](#ref-eilbrecht-stursberg-2020), and an equivalent position representation appears in [[2, §3.1, Eq. (1)]](#ref-yoneda-et-al-2018). The longitudinal and lateral state equations under the same coordinate convention appear in [[3, §4.3, Eqs. (4.33a)–(4.33b)]](#ref-ljungqvist-2020).

### Heading-error kinematics

The heading error is defined by

$$
\varphi(t) =
\theta(t) -
\theta_r\bigl(s(t)\bigr).
$$

Apply the chain rule with respect to time and substitute $\kappa_r=\mathrm{d}\theta_r/\mathrm{d}s$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dot\varphi
&=
\dot\theta -
\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta_r}{\mathrm{d}s}\dot s\\[0.6em]
&=
\dot\theta -
\kappa_r\dot s.
\end{aligned}
$$

On an interval where $v\ne0$, using $\kappa_v=\mathrm{d}\theta/\mathrm{d}\ell$ and $\mathrm{d}\ell/\mathrm{d}t=v$ gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dot\theta
&=
\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{\mathrm{d}\ell}\frac{\mathrm{d}\ell}{\mathrm{d}t}\\[0.6em]
&=
v\kappa_v.
\end{aligned}
$$

When $v=0$, $\ell$ cannot locally replace $t$ as a parameter at that instant. If the curvature input $\kappa_v$ is bounded, however, the kinematic relation $\dot\theta=v\kappa_v$ extends continuously to the stationary state, where $\dot\theta=0$.

The heading-error kinematics are therefore

$$
\dot\varphi =
v\kappa_v -
\kappa_r\dot s.
$$

### Three-state time-domain model

Combining the heading-error kinematics with the position-coordinate kinematics gives the three-state time-domain model for $(s,d,\varphi)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dot s
&=
\frac{v\cos\varphi}{1-d\kappa_r},\\[0.65em]
\dot d
&=
v\sin\varphi,\\[0.65em]
\dot\varphi
&=
v\kappa_v -
\dot s\,\kappa_r\\[0.65em]
&=
v\left(
\kappa_v-\frac{\kappa_r\cos\varphi}{1-d\kappa_r}
\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$

Here, $\kappa_r=\kappa_r(s)$ is determined by the reference path. The signed longitudinal velocity $v$ and vehicle-trajectory curvature $\kappa_v$ are treated as inputs to the kinematic model. In a particular steering model, $\kappa_v$ is normally determined in turn by the steering geometry. Model validity requires both a unique closest-point projection and $1-d\kappa_r\ne0$. The former gives the vehicle position a unique $(s,d)$ representation, while the latter is the local regularity condition that prevents the normal-coordinate map from degenerating in the tangential direction. The region satisfying both conditions is called the Frenet-coordinate validity domain here. A standard Frenet vehicle model corresponding to Equation (2) appears in [[3, §4.3, Eq. (4.41)]](#ref-ljungqvist-2020).

### Reversing in the time-domain model

Equation (2) applies to reversing without a separate derivation. The time-domain derivation already defines $v$ as the signed longitudinal velocity positive along the heading unit vector $\mathbf e_\theta$ and uses the same no-sideslip kinematics:

$$
\dot{\mathbf z}=v\mathbf e_\theta,
\qquad
\dot\theta=v\kappa_v.
$$

Neither relation requires $v>0$. When driving forward, $v>0$ and the velocity vector $\dot{\mathbf z}$ points in the same direction as $\mathbf e_\theta$. When reversing, $v<0$ and $\dot{\mathbf z}$ points in the opposite direction. Differentiating the position-coordinate relation (1) with respect to time, comparing components in the Frenet frame, and applying the chain rule to the heading error are all independent of the sign of $v$. The same three-state model therefore remains valid in reverse.

Let $\operatorname{sgn}(x)$ denote the sign function, whose value is respectively $1$, $0$, or $-1$ when $x>0$, $x=0$, or $x<0$. From Equation (2), the direction of change of the reference-path arc-length coordinate generally satisfies

$$
\operatorname{sgn}(\dot s)=
\operatorname{sgn}(v)
\operatorname{sgn}\!\left(\frac{\cos\varphi}{1-d\kappa_r}\right).
$$

In the usual Frenet tracking region where $1-d\kappa_r>0$ and $|\varphi|<\pi/2$, forward motion has $\dot s>0$ and reversing has $\dot s<0$. More generally, the sign must be determined from the equation above; “reversing” alone does not determine the sign of $\dot s$ independently of $\varphi$ and $1-d\kappa_r$.

For a strict comparison, fix the same state $(s,d,\varphi)$, the same vehicle curvature $\kappa_v$, and the same speed magnitude $u>0$, changing only the longitudinal velocity from forward $v=u$ to reverse $v=-u$:

| Quantity                                                        | Forward: $v=u$                                                    | Reverse: $v=-u$                                                    | Definition or model changed?           |
| --------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------- |
| Vehicle velocity $\dot{\mathbf z}$                              | $u\mathbf e_\theta$                                               | $-u\mathbf e_\theta$                                               | No-sideslip relation unchanged         |
| Oriented arc-length rate $\mathrm{d}\ell/\mathrm{d}t$           | $u$                                                               | $-u$                                                               | Always $\mathrm{d}\ell/\mathrm{d}t=v$  |
| Reference-path coordinate rate $\dot s$                         | $\dfrac{u\cos\varphi}{1-d\kappa_r}$                               | $-\dfrac{u\cos\varphi}{1-d\kappa_r}$                               | Determined by the same state equation  |
| Lateral-deviation rate $\dot d$                                 | $u\sin\varphi$                                                    | $-u\sin\varphi$                                                    | Definition of $d$ unchanged            |
| Heading rate $\dot\theta$                                       | $u\kappa_v$                                                       | $-u\kappa_v$                                                       | $\kappa_v$ is not artificially negated |
| Heading-error rate $\dot\varphi$                                | $u\left(\kappa_v-\dfrac{\kappa_r\cos\varphi}{1-d\kappa_r}\right)$ | $-u\left(\kappa_v-\dfrac{\kappa_r\cos\varphi}{1-d\kappa_r}\right)$ | Definition of $\varphi$ unchanged      |
| Reference curvature $\kappa_r$ and vehicle curvature $\kappa_v$ | Existing sign conventions                                         | Same sign conventions                                              | Neither is modified                    |
| Equation (2)                                                    | Original equations                                                | Original equations                                                 | Completely unchanged                   |

Under these comparison conditions, changing only the sign of $v$ reverses all three state derivatives in Equation (2). Time $t$ continues to advance in its positive direction. Unlike reversing in the reference-path-arc-length-domain model, time-domain reversing is represented by $v<0$, not by integrating time backward. When stationary, $v=0$, and Equation (2) gives $\dot s=\dot d=\dot\varphi=0$.

## Reference-path-arc-length-domain Frenet kinematics

This section constructs the model directly from the arc-length-parameterized position-coordinate relation and heading-error relation. It neither invokes the time-domain state equations nor uses time derivatives.

### Oriented arc-length coordinate of the vehicle trajectory

The variable $s$ is the arc-length coordinate of the projection point on the reference path. The variable $\ell$ is the oriented arc-length coordinate of the rear-axle-center trajectory, positive along the vehicle heading unit vector $\mathbf e_\theta$. On a nonstationary trajectory segment where the Frenet coordinates are valid, $s$ and $d$ can both be treated as functions of $\ell$, so Equation (1) becomes

$$
\mathbf z(\ell) =
\mathbf r\bigl(s(\ell)\bigr)
+
d(\ell)\mathbf N\bigl(s(\ell)\bigr).
$$

Differentiate directly with respect to $\ell$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf z}{\mathrm{d}\ell}
&=
\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf r}{\mathrm{d}s}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\mathrm{d}\ell}
+
\frac{\mathrm{d}\,d}{\mathrm{d}\ell}\mathbf N
+
d\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf N}{\mathrm{d}s}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\mathrm{d}\ell}\\[0.8em]
&=
\mathbf T\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\mathrm{d}\ell}
+
\frac{\mathrm{d}\,d}{\mathrm{d}\ell}\mathbf N
+
d\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf N}{\mathrm{d}s}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\mathrm{d}\ell}.
\end{aligned}
$$

From the Frenet relation $\mathrm{d}\mathbf N/\mathrm{d}s=-\kappa_r\mathbf T$ and $\langle\mathbf T,\mathbf N\rangle=0$,

$$
\left\langle
\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf N}{\mathrm{d}s},
\mathbf N
\right\rangle
=-\kappa_r\langle\mathbf T,\mathbf N\rangle=0.
$$

Project the position derivative onto $\mathbf N$ and use $\langle\mathbf T,\mathbf N\rangle=0$ and $\langle\mathbf N,\mathbf N\rangle=1$:

$$
\left\langle
\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf z}{\mathrm{d}\ell},
\mathbf N
\right\rangle =
\frac{\mathrm{d}\,d}{\mathrm{d}\ell}.
$$

By the orientation of $\ell$ and the no-sideslip constraint, the unit tangent of the vehicle trajectory with respect to $\ell$ is the heading unit vector:

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf z}{\mathrm{d}\ell} =
\mathbf e_\theta =
\cos\varphi\,\mathbf T
+
\sin\varphi\,\mathbf N.
$$

Its normal component is

$$
\left\langle
\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf z}{\mathrm{d}\ell},
\mathbf N
\right\rangle =
\sin\varphi.
$$

Equating the same normal component gives

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}\,d}{\mathrm{d}\ell} =
\sin\varphi =
\sin(\theta-\theta_r).
$$

This result follows directly from the position relation parameterized by $\ell$ and is not inferred backward from time derivatives. Reference-path curvature does not appear explicitly because the projection of $\mathrm{d}\mathbf N/\mathrm{d}s=-\kappa_r\mathbf T$ onto the $\mathbf N$ direction is zero. General regularity of the closest-point map within a tubular neighborhood is discussed in [[4, §2.1, Eq. (4), Proposition 2 and Theorem 3]](#ref-kublik-tsai-2016).

### Local relationship between reference-path and vehicle-trajectory arc lengths

Substituting $\mathrm{d}\mathbf N/\mathrm{d}s=-\kappa_r\mathbf T$ into the position derivative from the previous subsection gives

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf z}{\mathrm{d}\ell} =
(1-d\kappa_r)\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\mathrm{d}\ell}\mathbf T
+
\frac{\mathrm{d}\,d}{\mathrm{d}\ell}\mathbf N.
$$

At the same time, the vehicle-trajectory unit tangent with respect to oriented arc length $\ell$ is

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf z}{\mathrm{d}\ell} =
\cos\varphi\,\mathbf T
+
\sin\varphi\,\mathbf N.
$$

Equating tangential components gives

$$
(1-d\kappa_r)\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\mathrm{d}\ell} =
\cos\varphi.
$$

When $1-d\kappa_r\ne0$ and $\cos\varphi\ne0$, this equation gives $\mathrm{d}s/\mathrm{d}\ell\ne0$, so $s$ and $\ell$ are locally interchangeable parameters. “Locally interchangeable” means that, near the current point, the same vehicle-trajectory segment can be described using either $s$ or $\ell$, with a differentiable local inverse transformation between them. Their local arc-length relationship is

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}\ell}{\mathrm{d}s} =
\frac{1-d\kappa_r}{\cos\varphi}.
\tag{3}
$$

The inverse relation is

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\mathrm{d}\ell} =
\frac{\cos\varphi}{1-d\kappa_r}.
$$

For an interactive examination of how $d$, $\kappa_r$, and $\varphi$ jointly affect the local arc-length relationship in Equation (3), together with the Frenet geometric configurations corresponding to degeneration of the coordinate map and failure of the parameterization, see [Frenet Arc-Length Conversion](../frenet-arc-length-conversion/).

### Lateral-deviation equation

On an interval where $\mathrm{d}s/\mathrm{d}\ell\ne0$, the chain rule gives

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}\,d}{\mathrm{d}\ell} =
\frac{\mathrm{d}\,d}{\mathrm{d}s}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\mathrm{d}\ell}.
$$

Substituting $\mathrm{d}\,d/\mathrm{d}\ell=\sin\varphi$ and the inverse of Equation (3) yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mathrm{d}\,d}{\mathrm{d}s}
&=
\frac{\sin\varphi}
{\cos\varphi/(1-d\kappa_r)}\\[0.6em]
&=
(1-d\kappa_r)\tan\varphi.
\end{aligned}
$$

The relation $\mathrm{d}\,d/\mathrm{d}\ell=\sin\varphi$ contains no explicit reference-path curvature because it describes normal displacement per unit oriented vehicle-trajectory arc length. By contrast, $\mathrm{d}\,d/\mathrm{d}s$ explicitly contains $\kappa_r$, because changing the independent variable to the reference-path arc-length coordinate requires the inverse of Equation (3) for the scale conversion.

### Heading-error equation

Under the conditions for Equation (3), $s$ and $\ell$ are locally interchangeable parameters. Along the same vehicle trajectory, write the heading error directly as

$$
\varphi(s)=\theta\bigl(\ell(s)\bigr)-\theta_r(s).
$$

Differentiate with respect to reference-path arc length $s$, using $\kappa_v=\mathrm{d}\theta/\mathrm{d}\ell$, $\kappa_r=\mathrm{d}\theta_r/\mathrm{d}s$, and Equation (3):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mathrm{d}\varphi}{\mathrm{d}s}
&=
\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{\mathrm{d}\ell}\frac{\mathrm{d}\ell}{\mathrm{d}s}-\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta_r}{\mathrm{d}s}\\[0.65em]
&=
\kappa_v\frac{1-d\kappa_r}{\cos\varphi}-\kappa_r.
\end{aligned}
$$

The first term is the rate of change of vehicle heading per unit reference-path arc length, while the second term, $\kappa_r=\mathrm{d}\theta_r/\mathrm{d}s$, is the rate of change of reference heading under the same independent variable.

### Two-state reference-path-arc-length-domain model

Combining the lateral-deviation and heading-error equations gives the spatial-domain model with independent variable $s$ and state $(d,\varphi)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mathrm{d}\,d}{\mathrm{d}s}
&=
(1-d\kappa_r)\tan\varphi,\\[0.7em]
\frac{\mathrm{d}\varphi}{\mathrm{d}s}
&=
\frac{(1-d\kappa_r)\kappa_v}{\cos\varphi}-\kappa_r.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$

Here, $\kappa_r=\kappa_r(s)$ is determined by the reference path, and $\kappa_v$ is the vehicle-trajectory curvature input. Equation (4) follows entirely from arc-length-parameterized geometric relations and does not depend on the time-domain model. In addition to a unique closest-point projection and $1-d\kappa_r\ne0$, it requires $\cos\varphi\ne0$. Otherwise, $\mathrm{d}s/\mathrm{d}\ell=0$, and the reference-path arc-length coordinate $s$ cannot serve as a local independent variable of the vehicle trajectory at that point.

### Reversing in the arc-length-domain model

Equation (4) also applies to reversing without a separate derivation. The reason is that the preceding derivation uses the oriented arc-length coordinate $\ell$, positive along the vehicle heading unit vector, and relies only on the no-sideslip geometric differential relations

$$
\mathrm{d}\mathbf z=\mathbf e_\theta\,\mathrm{d}\ell,
\qquad
\mathrm{d}\theta=\kappa_v\,\mathrm{d}\ell.
$$

Neither relation requires $\mathrm{d}\ell>0$. For forward motion, $\mathrm{d}\ell>0$ and the displacement $\mathrm{d}\mathbf z$ points with $\mathbf e_\theta$. For reverse motion, $\mathrm{d}\ell<0$ and $\mathrm{d}\mathbf z$ points against $\mathbf e_\theta$, while $\mathrm{d}\mathbf z/\mathrm{d}\ell=\mathbf e_\theta$ remains true. The component comparison of the position differential, arc-length conversion, and chain rule are therefore unchanged in reverse, yielding the same two-state model.

The inverse of Equation (3) gives

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\mathrm{d}\ell}=\frac{\cos\varphi}{1-d\kappa_r}.
$$

In general, the signs of $\mathrm{d}s$ and $\mathrm{d}\ell$ are related by

$$
\operatorname{sgn}(\mathrm{d}s)=
\operatorname{sgn}(\mathrm{d}\ell)
\operatorname{sgn}\!\left(\frac{\cos\varphi}{1-d\kappa_r}\right).
$$

In the usual Frenet tracking region where $1-d\kappa_r>0$ and $|\varphi|<\pi/2$, $\mathrm{d}s/\mathrm{d}\ell>0$, so forward motion has $\mathrm{d}s>0$ and reverse motion has $\mathrm{d}s<0$. Given the same vehicle-curvature function $\kappa_v(s)$ and the same initial state, the same spatial-domain equations determine the same $(d,\varphi)$ state curve for forward and reverse motion; the curve is simply traversed in opposite directions of $s$. The right-hand side of Equation (4) requires no modification.

Within this usual tracking region, while keeping the same state, initial condition, and curvature input, the quantities correspond as follows:

| Quantity                                                                   | Forward                               | Reverse                                              | Definition or model changed?                    |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Oriented vehicle-trajectory arc-length increment $\mathrm{d}\ell$          | $\mathrm{d}\ell>0$                    | $\mathrm{d}\ell<0$                                   | Definition unchanged                            |
| Vehicle displacement $\mathrm{d}\mathbf z=\mathbf e_\theta \mathrm{d}\ell$ | Same direction as $\mathbf e_\theta$  | Opposite direction to $\mathbf e_\theta$             | Geometric relation unchanged                    |
| Reference-path arc-length increment $\mathrm{d}s$                          | $\mathrm{d}s>0$                       | $\mathrm{d}s<0$                                      | Determined by Equation (3)                      |
| Heading increment $\mathrm{d}\theta=\kappa_v \mathrm{d}\ell$               | Accumulates along positive arc length | Increment direction reverses for the same $\kappa_v$ | $\kappa_v$ is not artificially negated          |
| States $d$, $\varphi$                                                      | Evolve forward in $s$                 | Evolve backward in $s$                               | State definitions unchanged and not pre-negated |
| Reference curvature $\kappa_r$ and vehicle curvature $\kappa_v$            | Existing sign conventions             | Same sign conventions                                | Neither is modified                             |
| Two right-hand sides of Equation (4)                                       | Original form                         | Original form                                        | Completely unchanged                            |

Accordingly, when solving directly in $s$ within this usual tracking region, forward motion integrates from a smaller $s$ to a larger $s$, while reverse motion integrates from a larger $s$ to a smaller $s$. On a reversing segment beginning at $s_{\mathrm{start}}$ with monotonically decreasing $s$, a numerical implementation may define an increasing progress variable $\sigma=s_{\mathrm{start}}-s$. Since $\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}\sigma=-\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}s$, both state-equation right-hand sides then acquire an overall factor of $-1$. This is a consequence of changing the independent variable, not a modification of Equation (4) caused by reversing.
