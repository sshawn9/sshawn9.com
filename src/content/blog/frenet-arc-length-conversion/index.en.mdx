## Conversion relationship

Starting from the Frenet representation of vehicle position and the vehicle-heading unit vector, “[Frenet Vehicle Kinematics](../frenet-vehicle-kinematics/#local-relationship-between-reference-path-and-vehicle-trajectory-arc-lengths)” derives the local relationship between reference-path arc length and the oriented arc length of the vehicle trajectory in Equation (3):

$$
\frac{\mathrm d\ell}{\mathrm ds}=\frac{1-d\kappa_r}{\cos\varphi}.
$$

The interactive figures below vary the heading error $\varphi$, lateral deviation $d$, and reference curvature $\kappa_r$ separately and jointly to show how $\mathrm d\ell/\mathrm ds$ changes.

Here, $s$ is the reference-path arc-length coordinate of the vehicle's nearest projection point; $\ell$ is the oriented arc-length coordinate of the vehicle rear-axle-center trajectory, positive in the vehicle-heading direction; $d$ is the signed lateral deviation measured from the projection point along the left unit normal of the reference path, positive to the left and negative to the right; $\varphi$ is the heading error between the vehicle heading and the reference-path tangent at the projection point, positive counterclockwise; and $\kappa_r$ is the signed curvature of the reference path at the projection point, positive for a left turn and negative for a right turn.

## Using the interactive figures

Each relationship curve or surface shares one parameter set with the local Frenet geometry on its right. Move the pointer over a sampled point on a curve or a grid point on a surface to update the geometry immediately; on a touch device, tap a data point to select it.

All local arc-length conversion plots use the same visual notation: blue curves or gradient surfaces represent $\mathrm d\ell/\mathrm ds$; purple dotted lines or translucent planes represent $\mathrm d\ell/\mathrm ds=1$; orange dashed lines mark $\cos\varphi=0$; and red boundary lines mark $1-d\kappa_r=0$. The later plots of $1-d\kappa_r$ retain the blue relationship curve, purple unit reference line, and red degeneracy boundary. In the geometry view, increasing $s$ defines the positive direction of the reference path, lateral deviation is positive to the left, and counterclockwise rotation of the vehicle heading from the reference-path tangent is positive.

The geometry view treats the current projection point as its local origin. A line segment of fixed length $\Delta\ell$ represents the oriented vehicle-trajectory arc-length increment, while $\Delta s=\cos\varphi\,\Delta\ell/(1-d\kappa_r)$ determines the magnitude and direction of the corresponding reference-path arc-length increment. When $\Delta s<0$, the highlighted arc extends backward from the current projection point. The reference curve represents only the local curvature at that point. When $\kappa_r\ne0$ and $|\kappa_r\Delta s|\le\pi/3$, the complete highlighted arc is drawn. Only after the arc exceeds one sixth of a circle is it truncated, with an ellipsis shown in the correct direction; the osculating circle is not treated as the entire reference path and used to search for another projection point. When $1-d\kappa_r=0$, no finite $\Delta s$ exists and none is drawn.

The single-value sliders under “Experimental conditions” fix variables that are not represented by the current plot axes. The range sliders under “Plot window” change only the displayed coordinate or analysis range; they do not alter the mathematical relationship. Expand “Advanced range settings” to explore beyond the default limits. “Reset” restores all initial parameters and display ranges for the current experiment.

## Effect of the relationship between lateral deviation and curvature radius at the projection point

The term $1-d\kappa_r$ is the part of the local arc-length conversion relationship

$$
\frac{\mathrm d\ell}{\mathrm ds}=\frac{1-d\kappa_r}{\cos\varphi}
$$

that is jointly determined by the lateral deviation $d$ and the reference curvature $\kappa_r$ at the projection point. Within the domain where this relationship applies, once the heading error $\varphi$ is fixed, the complete influence of $d$ and $\kappa_r$ on $\mathrm d\ell/\mathrm ds$ is expressed through this factor.

Only the local relationship at the current projection point is considered below. Let the reference-path arc-length coordinate of this point be $s_0$, and abbreviate $\kappa_r=\kappa_r(s_0)$. For $\kappa_r\ne0$, define

$$
d_c=\frac{1}{\kappa_r},
\qquad
R_r=\frac{1}{|\kappa_r|}.
$$

$R_r$ is the radius of curvature of the osculating circle at the projection point, while $d_c$ is the signed coordinate of its center measured along the left unit normal. The center of curvature is located at $\boldsymbol r(s_0)+d_c\boldsymbol N(s_0)$, and

$$
1-d\kappa_r=1-\frac{d}{d_c}.
$$

The factor is therefore determined by the position of the vehicle's lateral deviation $d$ relative to the normal coordinate $d_c$ of the center of curvature:

- $d/d_c<0$: the vehicle and the center of curvature lie on opposite sides of the reference path, so $1-d\kappa_r>1$;
- $0<d/d_c<1$: the vehicle lies between the reference path and the center of curvature, so $0<1-d\kappa_r<1$;
- $d=d_c$: the vehicle position coincides with the center of curvature at the current projection point, so $1-d\kappa_r=0$;
- $d/d_c>1$: the vehicle lies beyond the center of curvature, so $1-d\kappa_r<0$.

The center of curvature here describes only the second-order local geometry of the reference path at the current projection point; it does not imply that the reference path itself is a circular arc. The relationship between $1-d\kappa_r$ and the nearest projection can be established directly from the distance between the vehicle and the reference path.

Fix the vehicle position $\boldsymbol z$, and let a candidate point $\boldsymbol r(\xi)$ move along the reference path, where $\xi$ is the candidate point's arc-length coordinate and $s_0$ is the particular coordinate of the current perpendicular foot. Define

$$
q(\xi)=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\boldsymbol z-\boldsymbol r(\xi)\right\|^2.
$$

$q(\xi)$ is one half of the squared distance from the vehicle to the candidate point. The constant $1/2$ does not change the extrema; it only simplifies differentiation. Because the reference path is parameterized by arc length,

$$
\frac{\mathrm d\boldsymbol r}{\mathrm d\xi}=\boldsymbol T,
\qquad
\frac{\mathrm d\boldsymbol T}{\mathrm d\xi}=\kappa_r(\xi)\boldsymbol N,
\qquad
\boldsymbol T\mathbin{\cdot}\boldsymbol T=1.
$$

Keeping $\boldsymbol z$ fixed, differentiate with respect to the candidate coordinate $\xi$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
q'(\xi)
&=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d\xi}
\left[
(\boldsymbol z-\boldsymbol r(\xi))
\mathbin{\cdot}
(\boldsymbol z-\boldsymbol r(\xi))
\right]\\[0.6em]
&=-(\boldsymbol z-\boldsymbol r(\xi))\mathbin{\cdot}\boldsymbol T(\xi).
\end{aligned}
$$

At the current perpendicular foot $s_0$, the vehicle position satisfies

$$
\boldsymbol z-\boldsymbol r(s_0)=d\boldsymbol N(s_0).
$$

Because $\boldsymbol N\perp\boldsymbol T$, substituting $\xi=s_0$ into the first derivative gives

$$
q'(s_0)
=-d\boldsymbol N(s_0)\mathbin{\cdot}\boldsymbol T(s_0)
=0.
$$

This establishes only that $s_0$ is a stationary point of the distance function. To determine whether it is a local minimum or a local maximum, differentiate again:

$$
\begin{aligned}
q''(\xi)
&=-\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d\xi}
\left[
(\boldsymbol z-\boldsymbol r(\xi))\mathbin{\cdot}\boldsymbol T(\xi)
\right]\\[0.6em]
&=\frac{\mathrm d\boldsymbol r}{\mathrm d\xi}\mathbin{\cdot}\boldsymbol T
-(\boldsymbol z-\boldsymbol r)\mathbin{\cdot}
\frac{\mathrm d\boldsymbol T}{\mathrm d\xi}\\[0.6em]
&=\boldsymbol T\mathbin{\cdot}\boldsymbol T
-(\boldsymbol z-\boldsymbol r)\mathbin{\cdot}
\kappa_r(\xi)\boldsymbol N.
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, at $\xi=s_0$, substitute $\boldsymbol z-\boldsymbol r(s_0)=d\boldsymbol N(s_0)$, $\boldsymbol T\mathbin{\cdot}\boldsymbol T=1$, and $\boldsymbol N\mathbin{\cdot}\boldsymbol N=1$:

$$
\left.\frac{\mathrm d^2q}{\mathrm d\xi^2}\right|_{\xi=s_0}
=q''(s_0)
=1-d\kappa_r.
$$

The term $1$ comes from the unit tangent length $\boldsymbol T\mathbin{\cdot}\boldsymbol T=1$, while $-d\kappa_r$ comes from the curvature-induced rotation of the reference-path tangent. Consequently, if $1-d\kappa_r>0$, $s_0$ is a strict local minimum of the distance function. If $1-d\kappa_r<0$, $s_0$ is a strict local maximum and cannot be the vehicle's nearest projection point. If $1-d\kappa_r=0$, the second-derivative test is inconclusive: whether the point remains a local minimum and whether the projection is unique cannot be determined from $d$ and $\kappa_r$ at that point alone. Even when $1-d\kappa_r>0$, it guarantees only a local minimum; global uniqueness of the nearest projection still depends on the geometry of the complete reference path.

### Positive curvature: center of curvature to the left of the reference path

When $\kappa_r>0$, $d_c=R_r>0$ and the center of curvature lies on the left-unit-normal side. As the vehicle moves leftward from the reference path toward the center of curvature, $d$ increases from $0$ to $R_r$ and $1-d\kappa_r$ decreases linearly from $1$ to $0$. When the vehicle is on the right side of the reference path, $d<0$ and the factor is greater than $1$. When $d>R_r$, the factor is negative, so the current reference point cannot be the vehicle's nearest projection. This region therefore does not belong to a Frenet coordinate state constructed from that point as the nearest projection.

### Negative curvature: center of curvature to the right of the reference path

When $\kappa_r<0$, $d_c=-R_r<0$ and the center of curvature lies on the right-unit-normal side. As the vehicle moves rightward from the reference path toward the center of curvature, $d$ decreases from $0$ to $-R_r$ and $1-d\kappa_r$ likewise decreases linearly from $1$ to $0$. When the vehicle is on the left side of the reference path, $d>0$ and the factor is greater than $1$. When $d<-R_r$, the factor is negative, so the current reference point likewise cannot be the vehicle's nearest projection. This region therefore does not belong to a Frenet coordinate state constructed from that point as the nearest projection.

When $\kappa_r=0$, the curvature radius at the projection point is infinite, no finite center of curvature exists, and $1-d\kappa_r=1$ for every $d$. Lateral deviation then has no effect on this part of the local arc-length conversion relationship, so no separate plot is needed.
